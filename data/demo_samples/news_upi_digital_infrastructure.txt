NEW DELHI: India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) processed over 13.1 billion transactions worth 19.78 lakh crore rupees in a single calendar month, setting a new benchmark for global real-time digital payment ecosystems, according to official data released by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) on Monday. The volume represents an annual growth of 54 percent in transaction count and a 40 percent surge in total financial value compared to the corresponding period last fiscal year.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) attributed this rapid adoption to the widespread deployment of interoperable QR codes across rural and semi-urban retail merchants, the integration of RuPay credit cards on UPI networks, and the introduction of delegated offline payments through the UPI Lite protocol. Person-to-Merchant (P2M) transactions accounted for nearly 58 percent of total digital transaction volumes, underscoring the shift away from cash in everyday micro-commerce across Indian grocery stores, fuel retail outlets, and public transport systems.

In an address at the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai, RBI Governor Shaktikanta Das highlighted that digital public infrastructure—commonly referred to as the India Stack—has democratized formal financial access across socio-economic tiers. Das noted that over 350 million active unique users in India now conduct digital banking transactions without recurring processing fees, supported by continuous uptime across more than 500 member banks.

Simultaneously, India's cross-border financial connectivity achieved key operational milestones following bilateral agreements between NPCI International Payments Limited (NIPL) and overseas monetary authorities. Direct real-time remittance linkages are now active between India's UPI and Singapore's PayNow network, enabling citizens of both countries to transfer funds across borders within seconds at fractional remittance costs. Additionally, cross-border merchant acceptance has been rolled out across retail destinations in the United Arab Emirates, France, Sri Lanka, and Mauritius.

To safeguard against digital financial fraud, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) alongside the central bank introduced enhanced multi-factor biometric authentication and automated suspicious transaction alerts. Financial analysts from the State Bank of India project that UPI transaction volumes will surpass 20 billion monthly transfers by the end of 2026, driven by artificial intelligence-powered conversational payments in regional Indian languages and expansion into international trade settlement corridors.
