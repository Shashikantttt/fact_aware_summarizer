BENGALURU: In a historic milestone for India's indigenous space program, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) successfully accomplished a soft landing of the Chandrayaan-3 spacecraft near the south polar region of the Moon. The critical descent began from an altitude of 30 kilometers above the lunar surface at 5:44 PM Indian Standard Time, orchestrated by scientists at the ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network (ISTRAC) in Bengaluru.

With this mission, India became the fourth nation in the world to achieve a controlled lunar landing after the United States, the former Soviet Union, and China, and the very first country to land in the rugged terrain of the lunar south pole. The Vikram lander module executed a precise four-stage autonomous braking sequence utilizing its throttleable liquid engines, safely touching down on the lunar regolith at 6:04 PM.

ISRO Chairman S. Somanath addressed the nation immediately after confirmation of touchdown, congratulating the multidisciplinary teams across the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre in Thiruvananthapuram and the U R Rao Satellite Centre in Bengaluru. Somanath stated that the mission demonstrated the maturity of India's indigenous sensor technologies, hazard detection cameras, and guidance software.

Following the landing, the 26-kilogram Pragyan rover rolled down the lander ramp to commence in-situ scientific investigations. Equipped with the Alpha Particle X-Ray Spectrometer (APXS) and Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscope (LIBS), the six-wheeled rover was tasked with determining the elemental composition of the lunar soil and detecting the presence of aluminum, calcium, iron, and titanium. Initial telemetry data confirmed that thermal sensors on the ChaSTE payload recorded surface temperatures ranging from minus 10 degrees Celsius to plus 70 degrees Celsius within topsoil depths of eight centimeters.

The total expenditure for the Chandrayaan-3 mission was approximately 615 crore rupees, representing one of the most cost-efficient planetary exploration programs globally. Prime Minister Narendra Modi, joining virtually from the BRICS summit in Johannesburg, announced that the touchdown site on the Moon would officially be named 'Shiv Shakti Point', while the crash site of the previous Chandrayaan-2 mission would be designated as 'Tiranga Point'. ISRO confirmed that all scientific objectives planned for the fourteen Earth-day lunar daylight period were completed ahead of schedule before the instruments were placed into sleep mode.
